The gap compounds at every step

Top reps get a few more opens, many more replies — and by the time it reaches the calendar, 8× the meetings. Not by working 8× harder: by writing differently.

Top-10% reps vs. the average rep (average = 1×). In meetings, that’s 23 against 3.

Part 02 — The email

The email, by the numbers

Six rules for the message itself. Each one comes straight from the data.

Subject line
<4

words in the subject line

Short phrases that look like an internal note beat full sentences and headlines.

Takeaway: “lobby refit”, “banquet seating”, “bodrum opening”.
Subject line
−12%

replies with an empty subject

No subject looks like clickbait. Lowercase, priority-based subjects win; buzzwords, numbers and name-dropping lose.

Length
<100

words in the body

Short enough to read on a phone without scrolling.

Takeaway: If it needs a scroll, cut it.
Length
3–4

sentences, total

Personalisation → problem → solution → ask. That’s the whole email.

Language
you > we

prospect-focused language

Top reps write far more “you / your team” and far less “we / our”. Pitch words measurably lower replies.

Takeaway: Count your “you”s and “we”s before sending.
The ask
offer > meeting

for the call to action

Ask for interest, or offer something useful they get even if they never buy — a quote, a sample, a moodboard.

Takeaway: “Open to seeing…?” beats “Do you have 30 minutes?”

Part 03 — The follow-up

The follow-up, by the numbers

Most replies don’t come from the first email. Six numbers on how to follow up without becoming noise.

Multi-touch
~6

emails over 14–28 days

Never send one email and stop. Replies keep coming through the sixth email or so.

Multi-touch
<0.5%

reply rate after that

Past four weeks it’s time to rest the account and spend the effort on fresh prospects.

Bumps
1

sentence per bump

Reply in the same thread with one line — “any thoughts?” — not a re-pitch.

Break-up
~2×

replies with break-up language

Asking for a clear yes or no nearly doubles reply rates on later emails.

Calls
2–3×

email replies when you add calls

A short voicemail that points to the email turns a cold name into a reply.

Personalisation
problem > fun fact

what to personalise on

Prioritise high-intent accounts, then reference a business problem — not their university or hobby.

Part 04 — Before you hit send

Pre-send checklist

Tap each line as you check your email. Eight yeses and it’s ready to go.

  • Subject is under four words, lowercase, and about their world
  • The first line is about something they’re doing this quarter
  • The email names one problem — just one
  • The solution fits in a single sentence
  • Under 100 words; no scrolling on a phone
  • More “you” than “we”; no seller jargon
  • The ask is for interest or an offer, not a calendar slot
  • A follow-up is already scheduled — call, bump, LinkedIn
